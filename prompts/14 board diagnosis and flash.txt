The board answers, and the USB-C question is settled by measurement rather
than by argument.

  0403:6010  SIPEED "20K's FRIEND"  serial 2023030621  (FT2232)
    interface 00 -> /dev/ttyUSB0    JTAG
    interface 01 -> /dev/ttyUSB1    UART, which is FPGA pins 69 and 70
  openFPGALoader --detect: idcode 0x81b, Gowin GW2A(R)-18(C)

So the on-board BL616 is still running Sipeed's stock debugger firmware,
JTAG over USB-C works, and bin/tang.fs loaded into SRAM over it cleanly.
Nothing ever had to be restored for programming; that half of the worry
was the documentation conflating two different things.

The pin 69 restore can also be checked from here, which I did not expect.
spi_irqn is int_out_n, and int_out_n is asserted while coldboot is set -
which, with no MCU attached to acknowledge it, is forever.  On pin 69,
the FPGA's transmitter into the BL616, a line held low is a permanent
UART break.  uart_tx instead idles high.  So the two bitstreams have to
differ at the moment of configuration, and they do:

  capture across configure          bytes on /dev/ttyUSB1
  old (spi_irqn on 69), round 1     00
  new (uart_tx on 69),  round 1     -
  old, round 2                      00
  new, round 2                      -

Two for two each way.  Repeated re-opens of the port with either
bitstream give nothing, which is correct: ftdi_sio reports the break edge
and not the level, so the signature is the transition and it appears only
with the old bitstream.  The first attempt at this test probed an
already-open line and found nothing either way, which looked like a null
result until the capture was moved to span the configuration itself.

That NUL is also a liveness proof.  int_out_n can only assert once
sysctrl has clocked out of reset, so the PLL locked and sdram2's horz
counter is running.

Not checked, and not checkable from here: the HDMI picture and the audio
fix, which need a display and speakers, and the MCU link, SD card and
keyboard - no dock is visible from this host.

The LEDs are active low, so a 0 is lit.  About a third of a second after
configuration a healthy board should show 0, 2 and 3 lit and 1, 4 and 5
dark: leds[0] is sys_rst_n out of reset, leds[3:2] are DCLO and ACLO
released, which means the PPU reached R177716 and started the CPU.
leds[1] is the beeper, leds[4] lights on a mounted floppy, leds[5] is
led_init from the MCU.

The board holds the new bitstream in SRAM only; a power cycle reverts it
to whatever is in flash.

lets flash new firmware onto flash

****

Written and verified by read-back.

  make flash-fpga-flash        erase 0x000000-0x0e0000, write, exit 0

Then the flash was dumped back with openFPGALoader --dump-flash
--file-size 917504 and compared against the bitstreams:

  new bin/tang.fs   907418 bytes of payload   MATCH, byte for byte
  old HEAD tang.fs  907418 bytes              differs at byte 238

with the area past the payload reading 0xff, as an erased flash should.
So what is in the flash is this build and not the previous one, and that
is a content comparison rather than an inference from behaviour.

The FPGA also reconfigures from it cleanly - two resets with the capture
spanning them, no break byte either time, which is what uart_tx idling
high looks like.  On its own that would not have distinguished a reload
from flash from an untouched SRAM, which is why the read-back is the
claim and the reset is only corroboration.

Still not tested: anything that needs eyes, ears or a dock.  Built,
linted, simulated, timed, flashed and read back are six different things,
and none of them is "the machine works".
