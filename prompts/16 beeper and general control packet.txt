The polarity of that is the useful part: noise while the machine is idle,
silence when it actually plays.  Every data-corruption theory predicts the
opposite, so it is two separate faults, and they were separated by
measurement rather than by argument.

The beeper - a real bug, and it explains "no beeps just silence" exactly.

xm2-01.v line 131.  R177716[12:8] select the tone and [7] enables it.  The
five terms were ANDed with an unselected divider contributing a plain zero:

  sound = R177716[7] & (clk8kHz & R177716[12]) & (clk1kHz & R177716[11]) &
          (clk500Hz & R177716[10]) & (clk250Hz & R177716[9]) &
          (clk60Hz & R177716[8]) || ~(|R177716[12:8]) & R177716[7];

Set one tone bit, which is what software does, and that clock is ANDed
with four constant zeros - the line never leaves 0.  The only case that
ever drove the speaker was all five bits clear, held high by the second
term.  Selecting a tone produced silence.  An unselected divider is now
don't-care instead, which keeps the author's AND intent, keeps the
all-zero held-high case that platform.md documents, and makes a single bit
sound:

  sound = R177716[7] & (clk8kHz  | ~R177716[12])
                     & (clk1kHz  | ~R177716[11])
                     & (clk500Hz | ~R177716[10])
                     & (clk250Hz | ~R177716[ 9])
                     & (clk60Hz  | ~R177716[ 8]);

The white noise - not the sound path at all.

A 1200 ms simulation with +AUDIODBG showed the mix dead at zero for all
23998 samples, and the HDMI decoder saw 59559 audio samples, every one
zero, with 0 ecc errors, 0 bad parity and nothing flagged non-PCM.  So the
encoder emits well-formed digital silence for the whole boot.

The board settled it.  With the OSD volume at Mute, top.v forces
volume_data to zero unconditionally - the samples are guaranteed zero
whatever the machine does - and the noise was unchanged, and it does not
get louder across 33/66/100%.  The noise therefore cannot be the payload.
It is how the sink reads the stream.

So the one outright spec violation in the encoder is now fixed: no General
Control Packet was ever sent, and HDMI 1.4b 5.3.6 says one SHALL go out at
least once per two video fields.  It joins the standing rotation as a
fourth entry, so ACR, AVI, audio InfoFrame and GCP each go out one line in
four - 7.8 kHz at this line rate.  SB0 bit 0 is Clear_AVMUTE; SB1 is
{PP,CD}, both zero for 24-bit colour.

Measured: lint clean; 479 GCPs in 60 ms with hdr 000003 and sub0 ...01;
audio unchanged at 0 ecc errors, 0 bad parity, and 2480 samples carrying
sound with L != R under +AUDIOTEST; memory ops byte-identical at ppu 5640
and cpu 14553, so nothing but the packet rotation moved.  make bitstream
builds with 0 setup-violated endpoints.  Flashed and verified by read-back:
907418 bytes of payload matching bin/tang.fs exactly.

What to expect is worth stating plainly.  The beeper fix should be
audible - it is a real bug with a mechanism matching the symptom, and it
is in the mix, which simulation covers.  The GCP is not a bet: it is a
genuine "shall" violation now corrected and it was the first thing this
repository's own notes said to try, but the Mute result proves the noise
is not in the payload and there is no evidence from here that the GCP is
what this television was missing.

If the noise survives, the next suspect is the video timing, and it is a
bigger job.  sdram2.v generates 1280x576-ish at 50.7 Hz, which is not a
CEA mode, so the AVI InfoFrame honestly carries VIC 0.  Plenty of
televisions treat a non-CEA timing as a PC input and route audio
differently or not at all.  Settling that means moving to a real CEA mode -
720x576p50 is VIC 17 - which is a display generator change, not an
encoder one.  Two cheap observations would narrow it first: what the
television's info panel reports for the incoming audio, and whether a
second sink behaves the same way.
