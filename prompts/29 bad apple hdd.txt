Found and fixed: the demo addresses the disk by LBA28 and the cartridge only knew CHS. Built, meets timing, flashed to the Tang's SPI flash - power-cycle it, mount bappwd.img as HDD 0: and boot from menu option 2.

How it was found. badapple's readme says the emulator it ships is UKNCBTL with "LBA28 support for IDE emulation" added and shorter DRQ timeouts. Block 2 of the image (un-inverted) writes 0340 to the head register before its reads: bit 6 set, LBA mode, with the sector number in {head[3:0], cyl, sector}. Our ide.v was written from the stock Hard.cpp, which ignores that bit and computes CHS from the same bytes, so every video sector came from the wrong place: coloured vertical stripes is what a 63x16 CHS reading of an LBA address looks like, and the Covox stayed silent because the demo never got its data. The image itself is fine: 63 sectors, 16 heads, inverted, which the MCU's heuristic classifies correctly.

Confirmed in the emulator before touching RTL: my scratchpad copy of the headless UKNCBTL got --cart and --hdd options plus the LBA28 branch in CalculateOffset() and NextSector(); with the WD ROM as cartridge and bappwd.img attached, option 2 boots and the Bad Apple silhouettes play. Without the branch it shows nothing.

The fix in tang/src/ide/ide.v: when curheadreg[6] is set the SD sector is {head[3:0], cyl, sector} straight, skipping the multiply stages, and next_sector counts the 28-bit number up into the registers so a multi-sector read continues and reads back right. tb_ide has four new cases: a two-sector LBA read, a carry from 0xFFFF into the cylinder high byte, bit 24 from the head nibble, and CHS again after LBA. It was not the DAC and not the mixing: it was sector latency. Fixed with read-ahead in the cartridge; built, meets timing, flashed to the Tang's SPI flash - power-cycle it.

UKNCBTL's Covox, checked again: value |= (port ^ 0xff) << 7, i.e. at the level of its beeper (0x7fff) and its AY sum, printer port 0177100 only, no 177372. Our proportions are close - beeper 8192, Covox 8160, three AY chips 18360 - so level is not what you heard.

What you heard, from the demo's own PPU loop, disassembled in the emulator at 001400-001672: MOV (R5),(R3) sends a word from the IDE data register straight to the Covox, MOV (R5),@0(R5) pairs write screen words, 52 samples and 102 pairs per sector, and BIT #10,(R4) polls DRQ between sectors. So the sample rate is the rate at which sectors arrive, and a wait for a sector is a hole in the audio. Measured with the port logged in the emulator: 1065 writes a frame with instant sectors (26.6 kHz - the readme's "~24 kHz"), 698 with UKNCBTL's stock 1 ms per sector (17.5 kHz); the samples are plain unsigned bytes, high byte zero, mean 128. Our cartridge started fetching the next sector only after the PPU had drained the current one, so each of ~500 sectors a second cost a full card read of silence, 0.4-1 ms against 2 ms of audio: a much lower rate and a buzz near 500 Hz.

ide.v now has two sector banks and fetches the next sector of a multi-sector read while the current one is drained, so the poll sees DRQ again at once. A READ arriving during a read-ahead discards it and fetches its own; a WRITE that fills meanwhile waits. tb_ide checks the second sector is fetched before a word of the first is drained, the third only once a bank is free, DRQ without BUSY across the drain, data in order, and the discard case; the earlier tests were taught that "last request" now runs ahead. Lint clean, 0 setup / 0 hold, Logic 48%, Register 27%, BSRAM 66% (one block for the bank).

Two WAVs went to you: demo.wav with the stock emulator's 1 ms stall baked in, demo_fast.wav without - the second is the reference. Docs: progress.md 17, platform.md IDE section, the CLAUDE.md trap and resource line. Nothing committed.
