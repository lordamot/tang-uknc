Found it, and it is one line.

The audio sample subpacket in src/hdmi/hdmi_tx.v had the wrong bit
layout.  A subpacket is 56 bits and carries two IEC 60958 subframes end
to end, 28 bits each: 24 sample bits, then V, U, C and P.  The flags
belong to the sample they follow.  What was there instead collected all
eight flag bits above both samples:

    {par_r, cs_r, 0, 0, par_l, cs_l, 0, 0, samp_r, samp_l}

so a sink taking bits 23:0 as the left sample and bits 51:28 as the right
one read this:

  left   bits 23:0   = samp_l                    - correct, by luck
  left   V,U,C,P     = samp_r[3:0] = 0,0,0,0     - parity always wrong,
                                                   channel status always 0
  right  bits 51:28  = {par_l, cs_l, 0, 0, samp_r[23:4]}

The right channel therefore came out shifted four bits high with its two
top bits taken from the left channel's parity and channel status bit.
Parity flips with the data, once per sample, so the sign bit of the right
channel toggled at random at the sample rate.  That is full-scale white
noise, and it is why the mix sounded like noise rather than like the
machine: the left channel was the real thing all along, the right one was
noise on top of it.

The fix is the interleaved order the standard asks for:

    {par_r, cs_r, 0, 0, samp_r, par_l, cs_l, 0, 0, samp_l}

The second half of the problem is why nothing caught it.  The HDMI
decoder in sim/tb/tb_top.v took l from bits 23:0 and r from bits 47:24 -
the encoder's own layout, not the standard's - so encoder and testbench
agreed with each other and disagreed with every television.  It reported
"2995 audio samples, 2976 with L != R, 0 ecc errors" while the hardware
was emitting noise.  The testbench now decodes the two subframes the way
a sink does, and additionally checks the parity bit and the validity bit,
which is what makes the check mean anything.

Measured after the change, 50 ms with the AY outputs forced to L=1234,
R=765 octal at 33% volume:

  hdmi: 7668 packets, 0 ecc errors (acr 639, avi 639, ai 639,
        audio 2995, null 2756)
  hdmi audio: 2995 samples, 2976 with sound, 2976 with L != R, overflow=0
  hdmi audio subframes: 0 with bad parity, 0 flagged not-PCM

and one subpacket dumped raw reads 80fa800814e000, which unpacks as left
0x14E000 - that is 1234 octal shifted left three by the mixer and then
eight into the 24-bit slot - and right 0x0FA800, from 765 octal by the
same route.  Both P bits make their subframe even, both V bits clear.

What is checked and what is not.  make lint is clean.  make sim carries
the numbers above.  make bitstream builds.  None of that is a television:
the claim is that the bits leaving the encoder are now the bits the
standard describes, not that a particular sink plays them.

The mixing you asked for was already in place and did not need changing.
top.v sums the two AYs panned ABC - left is A + B/2, right is C + B/2,
from aberrant.v - adds the one-bit beeper into both channels at bit 11,
applies the menu volume, and hands the same post-volume words to both the
I2S FIFOs and hdmi_tx.  The beeper and the AYs were already going into
the HDMI stream together; only the packing was wrong.

One thing left alone, worth knowing.  The samples are unipolar: silence
is 0 and sound swings only positive, up to 32672 at 100% volume, which is
sized so it just fits the positive half of a 16-bit word.  As PCM that is
legal - digital zero is still silence - but it has a DC component and
gives away 6 dB of range.  Centring it would mean changing the same words
the I2S path uses, so it is not something to do without knowing what the
DAC on the other end expects.
