I measured the two recordings and read the real module's schematic (aberranthacker/aberrant_sound_module, the PDF in the repo, 22 Aug 2023). Nothing in the tree has changed yet.

The recordings. Both are the same PT3 song at the same tempo: chroma cross-correlation peaks at 0.52 with tang.mp4 starting 14.9 s later, and they overlap for 18 s. On that aligned section, with the mid bands as reference, the Tang is about 4 dB lighter below 250 Hz and 11 to 14 dB hotter above 4 kHz. That is your "crisp, less bass": mostly a tilt with too much treble, rather than bass that is missing on its own.

The real module does have an amplifier, but it does not explain the tilt:
- Per chip, ABC stereo: A goes to L through 1k, C goes to R through 1k, and B goes to both through 2.2k each. All three chips share one 510 ohm load per side, so on each side A counts about 2.2 times as much as B. The real module is stereo, and the comments in aberrant.v and top.v that call it mono are wrong.
- The mixer is an LM358 on a single +5 V supply, biased at 2.5 V, as an inverting summer with 24k in and 10k feedback, so the gain is 0.42. Coupling capacitors of 10 uF against 24k give a 0.66 Hz high-pass. The 1200 pF feedback capacitors are marked NC, so there is no low-pass. The frequency response is flat across the audio band. The only thing to copy from it is the high-pass, which is the DC blocker we already planned.
- The beeper goes through a 510/510 divider (plus C17, value not printed) and 100k into both sides, which puts a full single AY channel at about a quarter of the beeper. Our 8192 against 2040 is the same ratio.
- The AY pins are source followers that can drive current out but hardly sink it. With nine outputs tied into shared nodes, a quiet channel drops out of the network and the loud ones take over. The real sum is therefore compressed rather than linear.

What the Tang does wrong that it can fix:
1. Aliasing. hdmi_tx picks one value out of the 3.13 MHz stream at each 48 kHz sample instant. In a model of that path, a 440 Hz AY square has out-of-tune products at -22 dB, 1.76 kHz at -16 dB and 3.7 kHz at -13 dB. A simple average over each 48 kHz sample period lowers them by 12 dB, and a proper decimation filter would lower them further. That out-of-tune content is the "crisp" part. It does not reduce the total treble much, because the square waves' own harmonics are real.
2. Mono. The real module is ABC stereo with the weights above.
3. No DC blocker, so a thump at every step in level. The real module has one at 0.66 Hz.
4. Optionally, the compression from the tied AY pins.

The remaining treble gap of about 10 dB above 4 kHz is not in the module's circuit. It is somewhere in the real machine's playback path. Before I tune anything towards it I need to know what the real machine's P3 output was plugged into.

This is done on the branch feat/sound-mixer, not committed. It is built, it passes timing and it passes simulation. Nobody has heard it on a board.

First, a correction to two earlier replies. The binaries I flashed were the committed ones, and both date from 3 Sep. The BL616 image does not carry the USB keyboard fix, and the Tang image is not a 14 Sep build with the AZ controller in it; AZ is on its own branch. Both binaries in bin/ are now rebuilt from this branch.

What changed:
- New tang/src/mixer.v on the PPU clock:
  - The module's resistor network: L = (33A + 15B)/2, R = (33C + 15B)/2, with the same peak as the old mono sum.
  - Its coupling capacitors, as a DC blocker at 0.95 Hz.
  - The new low-pass: one pole at 4.87 kHz, set by the recordings.
  - The volume and saturation, as before.
  - A 64-sample mean every 16 clocks.
- hdmi_tx.v now averages its input over each 48 kHz period instead of picking one sample.
- The two fifo_audio FIFOs are removed, and so is isread_aud, one of the two remaining clocks made from a data signal.
- Two OSD entries on the Aberrant form: Stereo Mono|ABC (letter o, default ABC) and Low-pass Off|On (letter l, default On). Both go through menu.c, variables_uknc[] and sysctrl.v.

Checks:
- make lint passes.
- The new make mixer-test agrees with values worked out from the schematic and the coefficients to within a few counts:
  - A alone gives 12618 on L and 0 on R, against 12622.
  - The DC blocker gives 6752 after 2^19 clocks, against 6754.
  - The low-pass is at -3.1 dB at 4.87 kHz and -7.7 dB at 10 kHz.
- ab-test, covox-test and hwen-test pass.
- The simulation with +AUDIOTEST gives stereo on HDMI and I2S with negative samples intact and 0 parity errors.
- menu-test passes.
- make fw builds, and make bitstream passes the timing gate with 0 setup and 0 hold violations.

Cost, against main built the same evening: logic 54% -> 59%, BSRAM 32 -> 30.

To try it: flash both bin/ files and power-cycle. Then toggle Stereo and Low-pass under Hardware > Aberrant while music plays.

Both clips play the same song (VDARK3.PT3) from 00:00 on the same BenQ monitor. I aligned them by their envelopes and compared each spectrum against its own 630-1000 Hz band. After the fix:

  1.6 - 10 kHz    2.5 to 4 dB lower
  10 - 16 kHz     10 dB lower
  16 - 20 kHz     24 dB lower (aliasing products near Nyquist that are gone)
  below 1.6 kHz   unchanged, within the noise of the recordings
  overall level   2.5 dB lower
  L/R correlation 0.90 before, 0.80 after, so some stereo is reaching the room

So the new bitstream is running and the low-pass is on. Anti-aliasing alone would not lower 2-10 kHz by 3-4 dB. The size of the cut roughly matches a 4.87 kHz pole.

Why it still sounds the same: the monitor's speakers already cut everything above 4 kHz by 12 to 25 dB before the fix, so the filter works almost only where they are quiet anyway. Between 400 Hz and 2.5 kHz, where they are loud, the change is small by design. The bass is set by the speakers too: the mixer is flat down to 1 Hz, and the real module adds no bass.

The first comparison, the real machine against the Tang, was on the television, and that is where the 11-14 dB excess was measured. On these monitor speakers the change is mostly out of reach. Better tests: toggle Low-pass live in the OSD on the same recording, or listen on the TV or headphones.

Added on the branch feat/old-freaks, which starts from main. Nothing is committed yet.

Old freaks is on the Aberrant form beside Stereo and Low-pass, where the sound settings are. It is letter g and Off by default. When on, mixer.v:
- adds a bass shelf: the signal plus a one-pole low-pass of itself at 244 Hz, which gives +6 dB at the bottom, +4 dB at 244 Hz, +2 dB at 500 Hz and +0.7 dB at 1 kHz;
- then applies +6 dB of gain to everything;
- then passes it through a soft limiter instead of the hard clip: straight up to half scale, a quarter of the slope above that, full scale at 81916.

Without the limiter, doubling a loud tune at 100% on small speakers would square it off. The gain goes to both HDMI and I2S, because both take the same mix.

Checks:
- make lint passes.
- make mixer-test: 50 Hz comes up 11.9 dB and 5 kHz 6.0 dB. A step of 18360 comes out at 22011, on the limiter's slope where the calculation puts it. The output saturates at +/-32767 without wrapping.
- make menu-test passes; the new entry is in the walk and fits the screen.
- make fw builds.
- The simulation with +AUDIOTEST is unchanged with the setting off.
- make bitstream passes the timing gate with 0/0.

Logic goes from 59% to 62%, about 530 LUTs, mostly the two 30-bit shelf accumulators and the limiter. Both bin/ files are rebuilt. The docs are updated: mcu.md, CHANGELOG, progress.md defect 21, CLAUDE.md and platform.md.
