****

Transients get through, sustained sound does not.  That is a receiver's
audio buffer drifting.

f_TMDS is 50.14 MHz and AUDIO_DIV was 1024, giving Fs = 48.96 kHz, 2% fast
and not a rate that exists in the standard, while the channel status block
declared a flat 48 kHz.  A sink clocks its converter from one and its buffer
from the other; 960 samples a second of disagreement empties or floods a few
hundred samples of FIFO in about half a second, and a stream that never
pauses never gives it a reason to re-arm - which is why clicks survive and
continuous AY music never plays a note.

No integer divider of 50.14 MHz lands on a standard rate - 48 kHz would need
1044.58 - so the sample instant now comes from a phase accumulator: add N
every pixel clock, take a sample and subtract when it reaches 128*CTS.  With
N = 6144 and CTS = 50140 the ratio is exactly N/(128*CTS) by construction,
so the rate the sink regenerates and the rate we deliver agree to the bit,
whatever f_TMDS really is - which matters, because f_TMDS is a PLL output
nobody has measured.  Verified in simulation: 9746 samples over 6363 lines,
203.0 ms, is 48,010 Hz, with 0 ECC errors and 0 bad parity.

Confirmed on hardware afterwards: hdmipkt went from +4883 to +4787 per
window, a ratio of 0.9803 against the expected 48000/48960 = 0.9804.

Then, with that build tested and the symptom persisting, the second fault.
volume_data_l/r were combinational, always @(*), and hdmi_tx latches them at
the audio sample instant - an instant with no relation to the PPU clock the
mixer runs on.  So the encoder could take the value while the adders were
still settling and send a carry-chain intermediate as a sample.  Nothing in
the diagnostics could see it: the probes sample the same wires on posedge
ppuclk_p, when everything has settled.  It was consistent rather than
intermittent because the old divider took a sample every 1024 pixel clocks
and ppuclk_p is clkram/16, and 1024 = 64*16 - so the sample instant sat at
ONE fixed phase of the PPU clock forever.  Registering it makes the sample a
settled value by construction, at 320 ns of latency.

The offset changed nothing, so sign was not the variable either.  The board
emitted, at 33% with S2 released: outmax 8780 outmin 7620, a clean 8192 +/-
580 entirely positive, and it was silent - while 510 +/- 510 from the raw
path played.  Comparable AC, both unipolar, one audible and one not.

So the next build stopped guessing and did two things.  The default became
the raw unipolar sum, the state that works, so the machine was usable with
no button held.  And S2 held subtracted a constant 512 after the volume
divide: same signal, same filtering, same amplitude, the only difference
being that samples dip below zero.  That isolates the sign on its own, which
no test until then had done.

Result: S2 held, no sound at all, both sources, every volume.  S2 released,
everything plays except beeper music at 33%.

So the working envelope, measured rather than argued:

  raw sum 0..N, quiescent exactly 0          plays
  raw sum minus a constant 512               silent, dips below zero
  DC blocked, bipolar, quiescent 0           silent, dips below zero
  DC blocked plus 8192, never negative       silent, permanent offset

Every silent case either goes negative or holds a permanent non-zero level.
The one that plays is the raw unipolar sum returning to exact zero - which
is what the real MC0511 puts out, with the television's own coupling
capacitor doing the DC blocking we were trying to do digitally.

Final state: the raw sum is the design, the DC blocker stays in the source
on S2 in case it suits another sink, and the beeper stays at 8192 because
16384 trips the mute at full volume.  Working: AY at 33/66/100, beeper at
66/100, no button held.  Remaining: beeper at 33% is inaudible at -24 dBFS,
and the beeper's mean still steps with the program material, which is the
defect the blocker existed to fix and is now lived with deliberately.

Two real bugs fixed on the way - the sample rate, 48.96 to 48.00 kHz with
the channel status no longer lying, and the unregistered mixer output.  Both
were found by the board and neither could have been found by reading the
source.  Nine things were cleared by measurement rather than argument: no
PPU bus timeout in any window of any capture, no dropped or stuttered HDMI
audio packet, the AY written and acknowledging and producing three channels
at full amplitude, and negative samples packed correctly per spec.

The lesson to keep: the probes sample the mixer synchronously and the
encoder samples it asynchronously, so the instrument agreed with the design
and both were wrong together.  That is the third time in this repository -
after the SDRAM model and the subframe layout - and it took three rounds of
the board disagreeing with the probe before I suspected the probe.

Operational notes: openFPGALoader -f -r writes the flash but does not
reliably reconfigure the FPGA, so a power cycle is genuinely required after
a write; and opening the serial port poisons the next flash, because the
BL616-emulated FT2232 will not accept the reset openFPGALoader does on open,
and --skip-reset does not help since the reset is inside the device open.
So replugs between cycles are unavoidable and RTL changes should be batched.
