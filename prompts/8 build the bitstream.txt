Fixed, and the fix was to stop believing this repository's own
documentation.

Every doc here, from before I arrived, said the bitstream needs the Gowin
IDE and that it cannot run on this machine.  Nobody had tried.  The
Education edition is a free 648 MB tarball, needs no licence file for the
GW2AR-18C, and ships gw_sh, a headless Tcl shell that does synthesis and
place-and-route without a window.  It is now in tools/ with everything
else, fetched by make toolchain, nothing installed on the host.

Getting it to start took four rounds with its own bundled libraries, which
are the sort of thing worth writing down because they will happen again to
anyone else:

  GLIBCXX_3.4.14 not found          its libstdc++ is older than its own Qt
  FT_Done_MM_Var undefined          its freetype is older than the system
                                    fontconfig
  ZLIB_1.2.3.4 not found            same story with zlib
  Cannot mix incompatible Qt
    library (5.15.18) with this
    library (5.15.14)               a 2019 Qt5Core sitting in bin/ next to
                                    a 2025 Qt in lib/, in the same install

All four are the same shape: the bundled copy shadows a newer system one,
or shadows another bundled one.  fetch.sh now moves the stale duplicates
into _shadowed/ and the Makefile wraps gw_sh in LD_LIBRARY_PATH,
QT_PLUGIN_PATH and QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen.

Then three things about gw_sh itself, none of which the IDE tells you:
its SDC parser takes no backslash continuations; ram_rw_check is called
rw_check_on_ram; and -use_sspi_as_gpio 1 is not optional, because HP_BCK,
HP_WS and HP_DIN sit on pins 56/55/54 which are the SSPI pins, and without
freeing them placement fails outright with PR2017.  The IDE keeps that
setting in impl/test003_process_config.json as SSPI = True, so
tools/gowin_tcl.py reads it from there rather than hard-coding it - the
same reason srcs.py reads the file list out of the .gprj instead of
keeping a second copy.

make bitstream now builds the whole thing in about a minute and copies the
result to bin/tang.fs.  Both shipped binaries are current:

  bin/tang.fs      7262008 bytes   30 Aug 2026
  bin/bl616.bin     430512 bytes   30 Aug 2026

The bitstream carries the four VM2 core fixes, the AY chipselect, the
stereo audio and the timing constraints.  Resource use: Logic 39%,
Register 23%, BSRAM 48% - up from 46%, which is exactly the second audio
FIFO the stereo needed - PLL 2/2.

And the .sdc has finally been run, which is the part I could not do
before.  It found things.

The first run reported 729 setup violations, because the counter-bit
clocks and five flops-clocked-by-data were being analysed against the
tool's default 100 MHz - signals that actually run between 3 and 25 MHz,
and event strobes that fire a few hundred thousand times a second.
Declared at their real rates, setup violations went to zero, with margin
everywhere: clk_25 needs 25.071 MHz and makes 106.612, clk_3_12 needs
3.134 and makes 40.105, the PLL's two outputs need 50.143 and 4.179 and
make 67.225 and 41.239.

Hold violations went the other way, from 30 to 559, and that number is an
artefact I could not get rid of.  create_generated_clock is the right
description of clk_25 and clk_3_12 - they are divides of the PLL output -
but this parser will not resolve the source.  Not [get_nets {clkram}],
because that name does not survive synthesis; not
[get_pins {pl1/rpll_inst/CLKOUT}]; not that with -master_clock either.  So
they are declared as independent clocks, the tool cannot relate the edges
of what is physically one counter, and it analyses the crossings at worst
case.  The .sdc says so at the top, in the file, where the next person
will read it: make the generated-clock form resolve before drawing any
conclusion from 559.

Two smaller things the run confirmed.  clk_6_25 cannot be constrained
because synthesis removes it - top.v brings sdram2's cpu_clk out to a wire
nothing reads, which is defect 5 in progress.md turning up in a second
place.  And bin/tang.fs and tang/impl/pnr/test003.fs are now genuinely the
same file, which they were not before: they used to be the same size and
date with different contents, and which one anyone was running had never
been established.

Docs updated to match, and that is most of a rewrite rather than a tweak,
because CLAUDE.md, the guideline, build.md, tools.md, progress.md and both
howtos all rested on the claim that this could not be done.  The guideline
now carries the general version of the lesson: treat "this cannot be done
here" as a claim with a date on it.

What is still true: nothing has been on a board.  Built, linted,
simulated and timed are four different claims and none of them is "works".
