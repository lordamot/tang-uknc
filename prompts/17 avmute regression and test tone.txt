Taken together with the Mute test the observations say something the spec
does not explain:

  samples == 0  (idle, or OSD Mute)  ->  white noise
  samples != 0  (player running)     ->  silence

That is backwards, and it also explains why the beeper fix appears to do
nothing.  Under the new code a key press makes sound a real square wave,
so the samples spend half of every cycle at zero; if zero is what produces
the noise, a working beeper sounds like chopped noise rather than a tone.
The fix may well be correct and simply inaudible through this sink.

So rather than reason further about the encoder, bisect the chain with a
known signal.  buts[1] on pin 87 is read by nothing else, so holding it
now replaces the machine's mix on the HDMI audio with a 1 kHz square wave
at +/-8192 - a quarter of full scale, and deliberately BIPOLAR where the
machine's own audio is unipolar 0..32672 with a large DC component, so the
tone tests that difference too.  The I2S path is left alone.

Verified in simulation before flashing: sub0 reads 82000008200000, which
unpacks as sample 0x200000 in both subframes - +8192 placed MSB-aligned in
24 bits - with V clear and P even, and 1984 of 2003 samples carrying
sound.

white noise when button not pressed, silence when button pressed

****

So a bipolar, spec-correct, quarter-scale 1 kHz square produces silence,
and digital zero produces white noise.  Held for ten to fifteen seconds it
stays silent, so it is not the re-lock muting that televisions do when the
audio stream changes.

That kills the DC-offset hypothesis and moves the problem firmly to the
sink.  What is established:

- The sink decodes our data island packets correctly.  The AVMUTE mistake
  proves it - a General Control Packet got through and was acted upon, so
  TERC4, the guard bands, packet framing and the BCH ECC all work on real
  hardware, not just in the model.
- The audio sample packets are well formed: 0 ecc errors, correct IEC
  60958 subframe layout, even parity, V clear, nothing flagged non-PCM,
  and the payload verified against an independent decoder.
- The samples are provably zero when the OSD says Mute, and the noise is
  unchanged and does not scale with volume.

The leading explanation left is that the video timing is not a CEA mode.
sdram2.v makes 1280x600 at 50.7 Hz, so the AVI InfoFrame honestly carries
VIC 0, and a television that treats a non-CEA timing as a PC source may
never enable its HDMI audio path at all.  The monitor volunteering
"recommended resolution is 1920x1080" is a hint it is not entirely happy
with the mode either.

Two cheap tests come before any more building: a second sink, which costs
no flash cycle at all, and the television's own HDMI audio format setting,
since one set to Bitstream or Auto can emit noise for PCM it misreads.
