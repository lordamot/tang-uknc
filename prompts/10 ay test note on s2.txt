What S2 does now

Press it and AY1 plays a note on channel A at full amplitude with a 6 Hz
vibrato; press it again and it stops.  Nothing is forced anywhere - an
injector in aberrant.v writes the chip's registers the way a program would,
so what comes out is the AY's own tone generator feeding the same mixer,
the same FIFOs and the same HDMI encoder that music travels through.  That
is the point of doing it this way rather than synthesising a tone: if this
is heard on the television and a PT3 module is not, the fault is above the
mixer; if this is not heard either, it is at or below it.

The mechanism, in two files.  top.v debounces the button over 2^20 cycles
of clk_25 - about 42 ms, toggling on the press - and hands aberrant.v a
level.  A sequencer there writes four address/data pairs, one bus cycle
each: R7 = 0x3E (tone A on, everything else off), R8 = 0x0F (channel A at
full amplitude, envelope off), R0 = the tone period, R1 = 0x00.  While the
note is on that burst repeats about every 10 ms, which is what carries the
vibrato; switching it off writes R7 = 0x3F and R8 = 0x00 once and then
leaves the chip alone.

Two costs, both written into the source.  The injector overrides BDIR, BC
and DI on the cycles it writes, so a program's write to AY1 can be lost
while the note plays, and those four registers are left holding silence
afterwards rather than what the program had put there.  And the level is
modest - one channel at amplitude 15 is 255, which is 2040 after the
mixer's shift, about -24 dBFS at full volume against the 18360 that nine
channels can reach.  Turn the menu volume up before concluding anything.

The octave

Writing the note meant picking a tone period, which meant checking what
period gives what pitch, which is where this fell out.  ym2149.sv reloads
its prescaler with {SEL, 3'b111} and toggles the tone output every TP ticks
of it, so the pitch is CE/(32*TP) with SEL tied 1 - half the
f = f_AY/(16*TP) that every AY note table is written against.  Measured:
the note at TP = 252 came out at 225 Hz, not 440, and the same module
simulated with SEL = 0 gives 450.

The 1.773364 MHz clock enable above it is right; SEL = 1 is the pairing for
a core fed CE at twice the chip's master clock, and this design cannot make
3.5468 MHz - CE is an enable on a 3.13 MHz clock.  So SEL = 0 is the only
fix and it is one bit.  I have not made it, because it moves the pitch of
everything the machine plays and that is your call rather than something to
slip in beside a debug button.  It is written up as defect 11 in
progress.md.  The test note itself is set to TP = 126 so that it is a true
440 Hz on the build as it stands; if SEL is ever changed it becomes 880 Hz
and stays perfectly audible.

And that result is worth writing down plainly, because it is the first
sound this port is known to have made through HDMI, and it closes a row of

the mixer - in what writes the AY, not in what carries what the AY makes.

What changed

Three lines.  dd2's BDIR, BC and DI now come through the same override dd1
has, so both chips get the identical burst on the identical clock.  dd3 is
untouched.

One consequence to be clear about rather than let you discover by ear: both
chips share CE and RESET and now receive the same writes at the same
instant, so their tone generators are in phase and stay there.  The output
is exactly twice one chip's - 510 rather than 255 - not two voices you can
pick apart.  So level is the whole observable here: it answers whether the
second chip reaches the mixer, and nothing about what it sounds like.  If
you ever want them distinguishable by ear, one count of detune on dd2's R0
would beat audibly; I left it out because a doubled amplitude is the
cleaner test of the thing you actually asked about.

100% quieter than 66% is the sink, not the design

It is the same offset-dependent attenuation the beeper trips at 16384,
arriving earlier because two chips at full volume put 4080 into the sample
rather than the beeper's 8192, and because the volume stage scales a
unipolar sum - a louder setting raises the mean along with the swing, and
this television responds to the mean.  What makes it worth recording is the
shape: the loudest setting is not the loudest sound, and nothing goes fully
silent at any setting.  So the mute is a gradient with a knee, not a
threshold, and 100% is already past the top of it.

Two things follow.  "Quieter at 100%" is the sink and not a fault in
whatever is playing, which is the sort of thing that otherwise costs a
build cycle to rediscover.  And the note is now a level ladder - 255 with
one chip, 510 with two, 765 with three - which is the cheapest probe there
is of where that knee sits.  Adding the third chip raises the peak from
4080 to 6120 at full volume, so expect 100% to get worse, not better.  That
is the measurement working, not the change failing.

Three rungs of the ladder now, and the shape is the finding

peak 510, sample 1020/2040/4080: plays, plays, plays but quieter than 66%.
peak 765, sample 1530/3060/6120: plays, quieter than 33%, brief sound then
silence.

Three things fall out of that shape rather than out of any single reading.

The loudest setting stops being the loudest sound well below full scale -
6120 is -14.6 dBFS, less than a fifth of what a sample can hold.

It is not a threshold.  The attenuation comes on gradually across the rungs
and only the top one goes fully silent, so the sink is riding the level
rather than comparing it against a limit.

And the mute takes a moment to engage - 100% at peak 765 sounded and then
stopped, rather than never starting.  That is the behaviour of something
integrating the signal, and it is the strongest hint yet at the mechanism:
what it integrates is the mean, and the volume stage scales a unipolar sum,
so a louder setting raises the mean along with the swing.  That is why
every DC-blocked form of this signal was silent and the raw sum is not, and
it is the first account of that we have had that predicts anything.

The rung this bitstream carries is 1530 - sample 3060/6120/12240 - so
expect it to be worse again at the top: 12240 is where 6120 already went
silent, doubled.  33% at 3060 is the one to listen to.  That is the
measurement working, not the change failing.
