What upstream actually does.  I read src/tang/nano20k/atarist.cst and
top.sv out of harbaum/MiSTeryNano rather than working from memory.
MiSTeryNano supports two MCUs at the same time and picks between them at
run time:

role                 external M0S Dock   internal BL616   BL616 GPIO
miso, FPGA -> MCU    m0s[0]  42          spi_dir   75     10
mosi, MCU -> FPGA    m0s[1]  41          spi_dat   76     11
csn                  m0s[2]  56          spi_csn   86     12
sclk                 m0s[3]  54          spi_sclk  13     13
irqn, FPGA -> MCU    m0s[4]  51          spi_irqn  69     14

The two FPGA outputs go to both ports unconditionally; m0s[3:1] are driven
z so a dock owns them.  The three inputs are selected by one flop,
spi_ext, which comes out of reset on the internal BL616 and latches to the
external one the first time m0s[2] is seen low.  That is why a dock needs
no build option and no jumper.

The firmware was already right.  mnano/spi.c is byte-identical to
upstream's firmware/misterynano_fw/spi.c - I diffed it - GPIO
12/13/10/11/14 and all.  The MCU side was never the divergence; only the
FPGA side was, so nothing in mnano/ changed and bin/bl616.bin is
untouched and still current.

What moved on the FPGA.  Upstream's m0s pins are 51, 54 and 56, which is
exactly where HP_BCK, HP_DIN and PA_EN were, and 55 (HP_WS) is upstream's
bl616_mon_rx.  All four audio pins.  This core has no HDMI audio, so I2S
is the only sound path there is.

                 before                 now
MCU link         71 72 73 74 75         42 41 56 54 51
audio I2S        56 55 54 51            71 72 73 74
serial           69 out  70 in          48 out  55 in

Audio swapped onto the pins the MCU link vacated.  The serial port could
not stay where it was: pin 69 is the FPGA's TX into the on-board BL616 and
upstream spends it on spi_irqn, and pin 70 is that BL616's TX, which an
external adapter would fight.  Both ends therefore move to 48 and 55, the
header pair upstream reserves for a USB-UART adapter.  The consequence is
real and worth saying plainly: the VP-65 serial port no longer reaches the
USB-C port at all.
