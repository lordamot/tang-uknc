Two hypotheses fitted, and they were distinguishable at the board in about
a minute.

A - the reset is never released.  pp_rst is system_reset[0], and main.c
line 288 asserts R=3 the moment the FPGA answers.  menu_init releases it
with sys_set_val('R',3) immediately followed by sys_set_val('R',0) - two
adjacent SPI transactions with no delay between them.  If the second is
missed the PPU is held for ever.  With no dock nothing ever asserts reset,
which is exactly why standalone works.  Simulation cannot catch this:
tb_top.v puts 50 us and two other transactions between its R=3 and R=0.

B - the mounted floppy.  mount_dsk feeds fdd4's disk_insert and the ROM
tries to boot from the disk.  The testbench never mounts a disk and has no
SD card model at all, so that whole path is unsimulated.

Answers from the board: the LED pattern is unchanged with the dock - 0, 2
and 3 still lit, so DCLO and ACLO are released and the CPU is running.
That rules out A.  And with the dock connected but nothing mounted it does
start, which is B.

Then, on the bitstream flashed earlier in the session, it started working.

The likely reason, though it was not confirmed before the investigation
was stopped: that build removed the spi_ext mux.  Before, spi_io_clk was
`spi_ext ? m0s[3] : spi_sclk`, so the SPI clock reached mcu_spi through
combinational fabric; progress.md already recorded PR1014 warnings about
exactly that, and noted that once the link became a mux "the SPI clock is
no longer a pin the tool can name".  With the mux gone the clock is pin 54
again and the tool treats it as a real clock - the new PnR report lists
m0s[3] in its clock summary.  A marginal SPI clock would break the
sustained sector streaming a mounted floppy needs while leaving the
low-rate keyboard and OSD traffic intact, which is the shape of what was
reported.  Hypothesis, not a finding.
