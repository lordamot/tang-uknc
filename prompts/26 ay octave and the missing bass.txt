Found and fixed, and it was not the HDMI path: every AY tone was an octave low.

The cause is one pin. aberrant.v tied the three YM2149 cores' SEL high. In ym2149.sv SEL selects the prescaler: 0 divides the clock enable by 8, which is what a real AY does (tone = fclk / (16 x period)), and 1 divides by 16, meant for a core fed at twice the chip clock - which is how the MiSTer ZX Spectrum drives it, a 3.5 MHz enable for a 1.7734 MHz chip. Here the enable is the chip clock itself, 1.7734 MHz, so every tone counter and the envelope generator ran at half rate.

Measured in a standalone simulation of the core, then in the unit test: with SEL=1, tone period 252 gives 220 Hz and an envelope of period 1000 gives 3.5 Hz; with SEL=0 they give 440 Hz and 6.9 Hz, which is what the chip does. A PT3 bass line at 65-130 Hz an octave down is 33-65 Hz, which a television's speaker cannot reproduce, while the rest of the tune an octave down still sounds like music. That is the "bass filtered out". Nothing in the HDMI path shapes frequency: the encoder takes the mixer word as it is, the resampler is a sample-and-hold, and the DC blocker is still out. The AY core itself is fine and does not need replacing; the flat tables and mixer are the same ones MiSTer plays with.

What defect 4 means. A timing tool checks every path from one clock edge to the next, and it can only do that for nets it knows are clocks. This design had flops clocked by things that are not clocks: a PPU register bit (step), one-cycle pulses out of other flops (clk_dsk, clk_dsk_n, sd_rd, sd_img_mounted), a compare (curs_set). Such a "clock" travels on general routing with whatever delay the placement gives it, the SDC could only call it a made-up 1 us clock, and nothing checked it. The paths were not wrong, they were untested, and each place-and-route re-rolled them. On top of that the MCU's 20 MHz SPI clock on m0s[3] was not declared at all, so the MISO path - the one the power-on handshake and every SD mount goes through - was never analysed either. Those two together are why a re-layout could take the start screen with an SD card in: if the handshake fails the MCU never releases cold reset, and no start screen means no floppy.

Done:
- fdd4.v runs entirely on the 25 MHz clock now. clk_dsk and clk_dsk_n are enables, the track ROM is clocked by clk_25 with the pulse as its chip enable, read_sd's asynchronous clear is synchronous, and step goes through two flops and an edge detector. step was the worst one: it changes on the PPU clock, which is the same counter as clk_25, so it moved at the very edge of the flop it clocked.
- top.v samples sd_img_mounted[n] on mist_clk instead of using it as a clock. sdram2.v's cursor flop runs on clkram.
- test003.sdc drops the three invented clocks and declares spi_clk at 50 ns with input and output delays against the MCU's sampling edge, in its own asynchronous group. The report now analyses it: Fmax 31.6 MHz against the 20 MHz needed.

Verified: make fdd-test, a new bench that pulls the old fdd4 out of git and runs it beside the new one - the words the PPU reads, the sectors requested and the head position agree across a spin, stepping past track 0, an empty drive and a motor restart. make lint clean. make sim boots identically to the unmodified tree, 15 frames at 300 ms; the one read-after-write miss it reports is present on the unmodified tree too, at the same time and address, and is noted as an open question. make bitstream: 0 setup violations, 44% logic, 48% BSRAM; the timing report's clock table no longer contains any data-signal clock. Nothing in the design is random, so a failure that a power cycle re-rolls has to be a state fixed for the whole power cycle. I read the start-up chain end to end - PLL, SDRAM initialisation, the reset counter, the PPU's reset, the HDMI serialiser, the MCU's handshake - and found four such states, all on the FPGA side. The MCU is not one: it polls for five seconds, sysctrl does not answer during its 335 ms reset, and its one self-reset on the coldboot flag is upstream's design.

1. The SDRAM was initialised 5.7 us after PLL lock, with no refresh cycles. sdram2.v's comment says "wait 1ms (32 clkref cycles)" - MiST's sdram.v, where the counter ran on a slow reference. Here it stepped once per 16 clkram cycles, so the 31 steps were 10 us and the PRECHARGE went out 5.7 us after LOCK; a JEDEC part wants 100 us of stable clock first, and the sequence had no AUTO REFRESH before LOAD MODE. A mode load the chip ignores is a whole power cycle of bad memory, and the screen lives in that memory. Now: 65536 cycles (1.3 ms) of settle after lock, two refreshes between precharge and mode load.
2. The HDMI PLL took another PLL's output as its reference and had no reset, so where it settled was a property of the power cycle. It is now reset until the pixel PLL reports LOCK, and the four OSER10s until the serial PLL reports its own. This case would show as no signal at the monitor.
3. The PPU ran from configuration. Its reset was the MCU's 'R' bit alone, 0 from power-on, so it executed its boot ROM before the memory existed and hit SDRAM with the 1801's bus timeout deciding what happened; the MCU's reset a third of a second later cleaned it up when it did. pp_rst now includes ~init.
4. init read as done from configuration until the first clock, because the reset counter had no initial value. The simulation found this - its shortcut waited for init and fired at time zero. The counter now initialises to 31.

The comment is in top.v, beside where the diagnostic tone used to be: "the SD and floppy paths are clocked by data signals that test003.sdc constrains at a made-up 1 us, so they are not honestly analysed and placement churn can break them silently." That described fdd4's data-clocked flops, which this morning's re-clocking removed. What it did not cover, and what broke the floppy in the build that fixed the screen, is the phase of the clocks I related this morning.

horz counts clkram edges from lock. horz[0] rises on odd edges; horz[3] toggles when horz[2:0] wraps 7 to 0, an even edge - the same edge on which horz[0] falls. So every edge of the PPU clock, rising or falling, sits on a falling edge of clk_25, 20 ns from the nearest rising one. -divide_by alone puts every generated clock's rising edge on master edge 1, i.e. clk_25 and the PPU clock rising together, which is wrong by one clkram period. With that model the tool checked every clk_25-to-PPU path for hold as if coincident - the false violations on fdd -> vp128, volume -> mixer, keyboard -> R177702 in the 09:51 report - and for setup as if it had 320 ns, when it has 20. The floppy's status word from fdd4 into vp1_128fdd is exactly such a path, and the 09:51 placement, which had never been asked to make it fit 20 ns, did not. That is the hang.

Fixed with -edges {3 5 7} on clk_25 and {17 33 49} on clk_3_12, which put the edges where the counter puts them. The report now shows clk_25 rising at 19.9 ns and the PPU clock at 159.5, the PPU-to-channel paths as the tightest in the design at 6.6 ns of slack, 0 setup violations, the false hold lines gone, and 20 hold lines of which 19 are the known artefact inside the SDRAM controller and one is a video register reset at 85 ps. The stale comment in top.v and the constraint file's own history are rewritten to match.

The 10:22 build is in the flash - one variable, the phase. Power-cycle, then load a floppy. If it still hangs, the next step is a synchroniser stage on that status word inside vp1_128fdd, which takes the 20 ns window out of the design rather than out of the placement.
